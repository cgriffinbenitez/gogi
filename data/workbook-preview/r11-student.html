Chapter 1 - Reading

Lesson 1: Setting adds meaning or style

ELA.9.R.1.1 · Key literary elements add layers of meaning

Skill Strategy

Point to the setting detail. Then finish: this place/time detail creates ___ because ___.

Common trap: Student describes where the scene happens but misses why that detail matters.

Question 1 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 69
So Apollo went down to Parnassus, and looked about for a spot in which to lay the foundations of his house. The mountain itself was savage and wild, and the valley below it was lonely and dark. The few people who lived there kept themselves hidden among the rocks as if in dread of some great danger. They told Apollo that near the foot of the mountain where the steep cliff seemed to be split in two there lived a huge serpent called the Python. This serpent often seized sheep and cattle, and sometimes even men and women and children, and carried them up to his dreadful den and devoured them.

1. How does the setting description in "savage and wild" help develop the conflict in the excerpt?

  • AIt shows that Apollo is unfamiliar with the land and must depend on the people who live there.
  • BIt presents the place as threatening before the Python appears, making Apollo’s decision to build there feel dangerous.
  • CIt suggests that the valley is peaceful but misunderstood by the people who live near it.
  • DIt emphasizes the size of the mountain more than the danger connected to Apollo’s plan.
Question 2 is based on the passage below
The Aeneid — Virgil · paragraph 23
“Near the Ceraunian rocks our course we bore; The shortest passage to th’ Italian shore. Now had the sun withdrawn his radiant light, And hills were hid in dusky shades of night: We land, and, on the bosom of the ground, A safe retreat and a bare lodging found. Close by the shore we lay; the sailors keep Their watches, and the rest securely sleep. The night, proceeding on with silent pace, Stood in her noon, and view’d with equal face Her steepy rise and her declining race. Then wakeful Palinurus rose, to spy The face of heav’n, and the nocturnal sky; And listen’d ev’ry breath of air to try; Observes the stars, and notes their sliding course, The Pleiads, Hyads, and their wat’ry force; And both the Bears is careful to behold, And bright Orion, arm’d with burnish’d gold. Then, when he saw no threat’ning tempest nigh, But a sure promise of a settled sky, He gave the sign to weigh; we break our sleep, Forsake the pleasing shore, and plow the deep.

2. How does the description of the night sky help explain the sailors’ decision to continue their journey?

  • AIt shows that the shore offers rest, so the sailors decide to remain there instead of continuing their voyage.
  • BIt suggests that Palinurus trusts the beauty of the night more than the practical signs in the sky.
  • CIt shows that the sailors use signs in the setting to decide whether it is safe to continue their journey.
  • DIt slows the action to create calm, but it does not affect the sailors’ decision to leave shore.
Question 3 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 71
Close to the foot of the steep cliff, and beneath the spot where Jupiter's eagles had fallen, he laid the foundations; and soon where had been the lair of the Python, the white walls of Apollo's temple arose among the rocks. Then the poor people of the land came and built their houses near by; and Apollo lived among them many years, and taught them to be gentle and wise, and showed them how to be happy. The mountain was no longer savage and wild, but was a place of music and song; the valley was no longer dark and lonely, but was filled with beauty and light.

3. How does the change from a “savage and wild” mountain to a place of “music and song” develop the excerpt’s meaning?

  • AIt mainly shows that Apollo chose a convenient place for a temple, without changing the mood of the land.
  • BIt suggests that the people move near the temple because they fear Apollo as much as they feared the Python.
  • CIt emphasizes the beauty of the new temple but leaves the earlier fear in the valley unresolved.
  • DIt shows that the place has been transformed from fear and danger into safety, beauty, and order.
Question 4 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 186
For nine days the heroes and huntsmen feasted in the halls of King OEneus, and early on the tenth they set out for the forest. Soon the great beast was found, and he came charging out upon his foes. The heroes hid behind the trees or climbed up among the branches, for they had not expected to see so terrible a creature. He stood in the middle of a little open space, tearing up the ground with his tusks. The white foam rolled from his mouth, his eyes glistened red like fire, and he grunted so fiercely that the woods and hills echoed with fearful sounds.

4. How does the forest setting make the hunt feel more dangerous for the heroes?

  • AIt shows that the forest gives the heroes places to hide, making the beast seem easier to face.
  • BIt makes the open space feel like an arena, but the beast’s actions are what create most of the danger.
  • CIt suggests that the sounds in the woods exaggerate the danger more than the beast itself does.
  • DIt makes the hunt feel dangerous by showing the beast disrupting the open space and filling the woods and hills with fearful sounds.
Question 5 is based on the passage below
The Iliad — Homer · paragraph 323
Meanwhile great Ajax (his broad shield display’d) Guards the dead hero with the dreadful shade; And now before, and now behind he stood: Thus in the centre of some gloomy wood, With many a step, the lioness surrounds Her tawny young, beset by men and hounds; Elate her heart, and rousing all her powers, Dark o’er the fiery balls each hanging eyebrow lours. Fast by his side the generous Spartan glows With great revenge, and feeds his inward woes.

5. How does the setting description in "Meanwhile great Ajax (his broad shield display’d) Guards the dead hero with the dreadful shad..." help shape the mood of the excerpt?

  • AIt makes the scene feel quiet and shadowed, helping the reader sense the character’s uncertainty before the action continues.
  • BIt suggests the character is surrounded by natural details but does not yet know what choice to make.
  • CIt makes the place feel familiar, which reduces some tension while still keeping the scene uncertain.
  • DIt emphasizes time of day more than the character’s inner uncertainty.

Lesson 2: Plot or conflict adds meaning

ELA.9.R.1.1 · Key literary elements add layers of meaning

Skill Strategy

Name the conflict or event. Then ask: what does this moment reveal, pressure, or change?

Common trap: Student summarizes the event instead of explaining the meaning created by it.

Question 1 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 153
Perseus drew his sharp sword and cut the chain which held her, and then lifted her high up upon the rock. But by this time the sea monster was close at hand, lashing the water with his tail and opening his wide jaws as though he would swallow not only Perseus and the young girl, but even the rock on which they were standing. He was a terrible fellow, and yet not half so terrible as the Gorgon. As he came roaring towards the shore, Perseus lifted the head of Medusa from his pouch and held it up; and when the beast saw the dreadful face he stopped short and was turned into stone; and men say that the stone beast may be seen in that selfsame spot to this day.

1. How does Perseus’s action against the sea monster affect the conflict?

  • AIt focuses on Perseus freeing the girl, but the monster’s arrival remains only a background threat.
  • BIt shows that Medusa’s power matters more than Perseus’s own decision in resolving the danger.
  • CIt shifts the conflict from rescue to victory by showing Perseus act at the moment the monster threatens the girl.
  • DIt makes the rescue feel sudden, but it does not change the central conflict of the scene.
Question 2 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 186
For nine days the heroes and huntsmen feasted in the halls of King OEneus, and early on the tenth they set out for the forest. Soon the great beast was found, and he came charging out upon his foes. The heroes hid behind the trees or climbed up among the branches, for they had not expected to see so terrible a creature. He stood in the middle of a little open space, tearing up the ground with his tusks. The white foam rolled from his mouth, his eyes glistened red like fire, and he grunted so fiercely that the woods and hills echoed with fearful sounds.

2. How does the beast’s arrival change the conflict in the excerpt?

  • AIt shows that the heroes are experienced hunters, so the beast’s arrival mainly confirms their confidence.
  • BIt emphasizes the beast’s appearance, but the real conflict begins only after the heroes attack.
  • CIt makes the hunt feel ceremonial because the heroes have just finished feasting.
  • DIt turns the hunt into immediate danger, forcing the heroes to react instead of simply preparing for action.
Question 3 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 69
So Apollo went down to Parnassus, and looked about for a spot in which to lay the foundations of his house. The mountain itself was savage and wild, and the valley below it was lonely and dark. The few people who lived there kept themselves hidden among the rocks as if in dread of some great danger. They told Apollo that near the foot of the mountain where the steep cliff seemed to be split in two there lived a huge serpent called the Python. This serpent often seized sheep and cattle, and sometimes even men and women and children, and carried them up to his dreadful den and devoured them.

3. How does the conflict involving the Python help develop the excerpt?

  • AIt shows that Apollo is entering a place controlled by a real threat, which makes his arrival feel purposeful and dangerous.
  • BIt shows that Apollo wants to build a house, so the Python is mostly an obstacle to construction.
  • CIt suggests that the people’s fear shapes the scene more than the Python’s actions do.
  • DIt makes the Python seem like a local danger, but not one connected to Apollo’s purpose.
Question 4 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 164
One day, as he was showing what he could do, he threw a heavy quoit a great deal farther than any had been thrown before. It fell in the crowd of lookers-on, and struck a stranger who was standing there. The stranger threw up his hands and sank upon the ground; and when Perseus ran to help him, he saw that he was dead. Now this man was none other than Danaë's father, the old king of Argos. He had fled from his kingdom to save his life, and in doing so had only met his death.

4. How does the accident with the quoit affect the plot of the excerpt?

  • AIt shows Perseus’s physical strength, but the stranger’s death is mainly background information. The passage points to a different effect.
  • BIt turns a public display into an unexpected consequence, showing how one action changes Perseus’s situation in a serious way.
  • CIt creates surprise, but it does not connect to the larger pattern of fate or consequence. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • DIt turns attention away from Perseus and toward the crowd’s reaction to the public display. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
Question 5 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 116
The beast sprang at Cadmus, and tried to seize him with its sharp claws. But Cadmus leaped quickly aside and struck it in the neck with his long sword. A great stream of black blood gushed out, and the dragon soon fell to the ground dead. Cadmus had seen many fearful sights, but never anything so dreadful as this beast. He had never been in so great danger before. He sat down on the ground and trembled; and, all the time, he was weeping for his two friends. How now was he to build a city, with no one to help him?

5. How does Cadmus’s fight with the beast develop the conflict in the excerpt?

  • AIt emphasizes Cadmus’s bravery, but the fight mainly serves as an action scene without a new problem. The passage points to a different effect.
  • BIt shows Cadmus defeating the beast, so the conflict becomes less connected to his larger goal. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • CIt shows that Cadmus survives the immediate danger but is left with a new problem, so the conflict creates both victory and uncertainty.
  • DIt makes Cadmus mourn his friends, but that grief matters more than the conflict with the beast. The development of the passage points elsewhere.

Lesson 3: Characterization adds meaning

ELA.9.R.1.1 · Key literary elements add layers of meaning

Skill Strategy

Point to what the character does or says. Then explain what it reveals and why it matters.

Common trap: Student labels the character with a trait but does not prove the layer of meaning.

Question 1 is based on the passage below
Romeo and Juliet — William Shakespeare · paragraph 80
JULIET. Tell me not, Friar, that thou hear’st of this, Unless thou tell me how I may prevent it. If in thy wisdom, thou canst give no help, Do thou but call my resolution wise, And with this knife I’ll help it presently. God join’d my heart and Romeo’s, thou our hands; And ere this hand, by thee to Romeo’s seal’d, Shall be the label to another deed, Or my true heart with treacherous revolt Turn to another, this shall slay them both. Therefore, out of thy long-experienc’d time, Give me some present counsel, or behold ’Twixt my extremes and me this bloody knife Shall play the empire, arbitrating that Which the commission of thy years and art Could to no issue of true honour bring. Be not so long to speak. I long to die, If what thou speak’st speak not of remedy.

1. What does Juliet’s speech to Friar Lawrence reveal about her?

  • AIt reveals that Juliet is desperate and determined because she demands a solution and is willing to risk death rather than accept the marriage.
  • BIt reveals that Juliet is calm and willing to accept whatever others decide for her. The passage points to a different effect.
  • CIt shows that Juliet is only asking the Friar for ordinary wedding advice. This does not explain the author’s move. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
  • DIt proves that Juliet has stopped caring about Romeo or her own future. The development of the passage points elsewhere. This misses the evidence in the excerpt.

  • BIt shows that Theseus is forceful and willing to punish the robber with the same cruelty the robber used on others.
  • CIt reveals that Theseus is unaware of the robber’s cruelty. This does not explain the author’s move. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
  • DIt shows that Theseus avoids conflict by leaving the robber alone. The development of the passage points elsewhere. This misses the evidence in the excerpt.

Lesson 4: Point of view adds meaning

ELA.9.R.1.1 · Key literary elements add layers of meaning

Skill Strategy

Ask: who sees this moment, what do they notice, and how does that shape the reader?

Common trap: Student identifies who is telling the story but not how that lens changes meaning.

Question 1 is based on the passage below
The Odyssey — Homer · paragraph 104
“He got more and more furious as he heard me, so he tore the top from off a high mountain, and flung it just in front of my ship so that it was within a little of hitting the end of the rudder.81 The sea quaked as the rock fell into it, and the wash of the wave it raised carried us back towards the mainland, and forced us towards the shore. But I snatched up a long pole and kept the ship off, making signs to my men by nodding my head, that they must row for their lives, whereon they laid out with a will. When we had got twice as far as we were before, I was for jeering at the Cyclops again, but the men begged and prayed of me to hold my tongue.

1. How does Ulysses’s first-person narration shape the reader’s understanding of this moment?

  • AIt makes Ulysses seem completely objective because he gives no opinion about his own actions. The passage points to a different effect.
  • BIt lets the reader see both his boldness and his reckless pride because he admits he wanted to taunt the Cyclops again.
  • CIt hides the danger from the reader by leaving out what the men say to him. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • DIt shows that Ulysses has no control over the choices made in the scene. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
Question 2 is based on the passage below
Romeo and Juliet — William Shakespeare · paragraph 22
ROMEO. O, she doth teach the torches to burn bright! It seems she hangs upon the cheek of night As a rich jewel in an Ethiop’s ear; Beauty too rich for use, for earth too dear! So shows a snowy dove trooping with crows As yonder lady o’er her fellows shows. The measure done, I’ll watch her place of stand, And touching hers, make blessed my rude hand. Did my heart love till now? Forswear it, sight! For I ne’er saw true beauty till this night.

2. How does Romeo’s point of view shape the reader’s understanding of Juliet in this excerpt?

  • AIt presents Juliet through a neutral description that shows no emotion from Romeo. The passage points to a different effect.
  • BIt shows that Romeo is focused on avoiding Juliet rather than noticing her. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • CIt presents Juliet through Romeo’s sudden admiration, making the reader see how completely his attention shifts to her.
  • DIt proves that Romeo already knows Juliet well before seeing her. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
Question 3 is based on the passage below
Romeo and Juliet — William Shakespeare · paragraph 84
JULIET. Farewell. God knows when we shall meet again. I have a faint cold fear thrills through my veins That almost freezes up the heat of life. I’ll call them back again to comfort me. Nurse!—What should she do here? My dismal scene I needs must act alone. Come, vial. What if this mixture do not work at all? Shall I be married then tomorrow morning? No, No! This shall forbid it. Lie thou there.

3. How does Juliet’s point of view build tension in the excerpt?

  • AIt shows Juliet is calm because she knows the plan cannot fail. The passage points to a different effect.
  • BIt removes tension by describing only what other characters think. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • CIt proves Juliet has already changed her mind about the plan. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
  • DIt gives the reader direct access to her fear and uncertainty before she chooses to take the vial.

The Odyssey — Homer · paragraph 106
“When at last we got to the island where we had left the rest of our ships, we found our comrades lamenting us, and anxiously awaiting our return. As for the ram, my companions agreed that I should have it as an extra share; so I sacrificed it on the sea shore, and burned its thigh bones to Jove, who is the lord of all. 4. How does the first-person narration affect the reader’s understanding of the return to the ships?

  • AIt makes the return seem peaceful because the crew no longer has reason to fear what happened.
  • BIt presents the event through the crew’s shared experience, so the reader understands both relief and continued danger.
  • CIt focuses on the sacrifice at the shore more than the crew’s emotional response.
  • DIt shows Ulysses receiving honor, but it does not explain how the crew experiences the return.

The Odyssey — Homer · paragraph 116
“Their hearts sank as they heard me, for they remembered how they had been treated by the Laestrygonian Antiphates, and by the savage ogre Polyphemus.   • BIt removes Ulysses from the action and focuses only on an outside narrator’s opinion.

  • DIt shows that the crew is excited and confident about being separated.

Lesson 5: Theme or tone layer

ELA.9.R.1.1 · Key literary elements add layers of meaning

Skill Strategy

Name the element. Then finish: this points to the idea/attitude that ___.

Common trap: Student chooses a broad theme or tone word without proving it from the element.

Question 1 is based on the passage below
Old Greek Stories — James Baldwin · paragraph 254
As for Daedalus, when the people of Athens heard of his dastardly deed, they were filled with grief and rage--grief for young Perdix, whom all had learned to love; rage towards the wicked uncle, who loved only himself. At first they were for punishing Daedalus with the death which he so richly deserved, but when they remembered what he had done to make their homes pleasanter and their lives easier, they allowed him to live; and yet they drove him out of Athens and bade him never return.

1. How does the people’s response to Daedalus help develop a theme?

  • AIt shows the tension between justice and mercy because the people condemn his selfish act but still spare his life.
  • BIt shows that the people approve of Daedalus’s selfishness and reward him. The passage points to a different effect.
  • CIt suggests that no one cares about what happened to Perdix. This does not explain the author’s move.
  • DIt proves that Daedalus immediately admits guilt and asks to be punished. The development of the passage points elsewhere.
Question 2 is based on the passage below
Romeo and Juliet — William Shakespeare · paragraph 7
ROMEO. Alas that love, whose view is muffled still, Should, without eyes, see pathways to his will! Where shall we dine? O me! What fray was here? Yet tell me not, for I have heard it all. Here’s much to do with hate, but more with love: Why, then, O brawling love! O loving hate! O anything, of nothing first create! O heavy lightness! serious vanity! Misshapen chaos of well-seeming forms! Feather of lead, bright smoke, cold fire, sick health! Still-waking sleep, that is not what it is! This love feel I, that feel no love in this. Dost thou not laugh?

2. How does Romeo’s language help develop the tone of the excerpt?

  • AIt creates a calm tone by showing that Romeo has completely solved his feelings.
  • BIt creates a conflicted, emotional tone by showing love and hate as tangled together in Romeo’s mind.
  • CIt makes the scene feel humorous because Romeo is mocking other characters.
  • DIt removes emotion from the scene by using only literal descriptions of the feud.

And AEsculapius proved to be a wise child, gentle and sweet and teachable; and among all the pupils of Cheiron he was the best loved. He learned the lore of the mountains, the woods, and the fields. 3. How does the description of AEsculapius help develop a theme?

  • AIt shows that knowledge becomes meaningful when it is used to heal and protect life.
  • BIt shows that AEsculapius uses knowledge mainly to gain power over others.
  • CIt suggests that healing is unimportant because people do not remember him.
  • DIt proves that AEsculapius avoids learning from nature or other people.

Medea — Euripides · paragraph 45

  • DIt shifts the excerpt away from family and focuses only on wealth. The development of the passage points elsewhere.

The Iliad — Homer · paragraph 203
Coon, Antenor’s eldest hope, was nigh: Tears, at the sight, came starting from his eye, While pierced with grief the much-loved youth he view’d, And the pale features now deform’d with blood.   • BIt removes emotion from the scene by focusing only on weapons and armor.

  • DIt shows that Coon is indifferent to his brother’s death and avoids action.
